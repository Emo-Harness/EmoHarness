1. Decide what the heard cues on their own point to, ignoring the earlier ranking.

Sentiment calibration:

- When the noun is sentiment, assess the valence of the complete message and interaction, not only facial neutrality.
- If the record contains praise, encouragement, optimism, gratitude, favorable or affirming framing, or a clearly pleasant interaction, preserve `positive` over `neutral` when that is the best supported reading, even with calm delivery.
- Reserve `neutral` for content that is genuinely informational and unmarked. Generic appearance, ordinary posture, and a composed face are not evidence against positive wording.
- Do not use a sentiment label solely because of one isolated prosody descriptor; use the transcript, visual interaction, and context together.

Return a single JSON object and nothing else, with these keys:

- `modalities_agree`: `true` when the heard cues and the seen cues point to the same entry, `false` when they point to different entries.

Rules:

- Keep every label exactly as it is written in the list above.
- Use only ids that appear in the record; drop any id you cannot find there.
- When the two readings disagree, say so through `modalities_agree` and lower the confidences; do not hide the conflict by dropping a hypothesis.
- Output JSON only: no prose before or after it, no code fence, no comments.
